Selected analysis

Two analyses, each framed as a question, a finding and a decision.

Profitability and capital structure

R&D investment vs. profitability

Question: Does higher R&D spending improve profitability, and how does leverage change that?

Impact: Evaluated 155 firm-year observations across 16 U.S. public firms and 8 industries (2015 to 2024) by building four panel regression specifications.

Insight: R&D showed a positive but conditional link to margin, higher leverage was linked to lower profitability, and returns appeared delayed.

Pricing drivers and model judgment

What drives tuition at small private colleges?

Question: Which institutional characteristics explain tuition, and can a model predict it reliably?

Impact: Built a regression explaining 90.6% of tuition variation (adjusted R²) across 128 private institutions using three variable-selection methods. Co-authored with two classmates.

Insight: All three methods chose the same six drivers, and I picked a slightly lower-fit model because its diagnostics were cleaner.

R&D investment vs. profitability

Why it mattered

R&D is expensed, so it cuts reported profit today for gains that may come later. Leaders weigh innovation against near-term margin, and the answer depends on how indebted the firm is.

The analysis

Tested whether R&D intensity and total R&D spending explained profit margin while controlling for firm size, leverage and industry. Compared pooled, panel, log-margin and quadratic-size models to see if findings held, and checked for omitted variables, multicollinearity and functional form.

Key insight

R&D was positively associated with margin in every specification, but conditionally. The pooled model reached R² of 0.87 (adjusted 0.86), while the panel and log models explained about 41 to 46%.

So what

Highly leveraged firms have less room to wait out the payback period, and judging R&D on one year of margin would undercount its value.

Analyst takeaway

A high R² is not the same as a useful answer. The weaker panel models were more honest about what drives margins.

What I would investigate next

How many years R&D takes to show up in margin, and whether that differs by industry. The pooled R² also leans on net income terms that overlap with margin, so I would test a version without them.

What drives tuition at small private colleges?

Why it mattered

Institutions set price against peers. Knowing the drivers shows which levers, like aid, faculty pay and spending, move with price.

The analysis

Started with 13 candidate predictors and let forward selection, backward elimination and best subsets compete. Then tested interactions by region and size, checked multicollinearity (two terms had VIFs above 9 and were removed), and tried five transformations to address non-normal residuals.

Key insight

Model 4 had a marginally higher adjusted R² (0.9072), but I chose Model 5 (0.9055) because its residuals best met regression assumptions. A model that fits well but breaks its assumptions gives unreliable inference.

So what

Holding other factors constant, a 1% rise in financial aid was associated with roughly $142 to $192 higher tuition (95% interval), and Southeast schools charged less than the baseline region. These are associations, not proof of cause.

Analyst takeaway

I would rather defend a slightly lower fit than rely on a number I cannot trust.

What I would investigate next

Whether the aid link reflects a discounting strategy, and whether it holds across more than one year of data.
